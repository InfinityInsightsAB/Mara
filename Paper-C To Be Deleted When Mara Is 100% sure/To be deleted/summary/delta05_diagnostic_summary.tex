\section*{Delta 0.5 diagnostic summary}
\textbf{Case.} $r=0.02$, $\delta_1=\delta_2=0.5$, $T=1$, $N_{\mathrm{ex}}=12$, $v_0=0.114$, $v'_0=0.110$. All outputs are under \texttt{D:/Mara PhD/Paper-C/To be deleted}.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The diagnostics do not support a stale benchmark or wrong-parameter explanation. The sandbox value for K=100, M=48, N=60,000 is 11.508319355383, and the manuscript CSV value is 11.508319355383; the absolute difference is 0.000000000000. For K=100, plain LSMC changes by 1.571 between M=48 and M=1200 at N=60,000, while increasing paths at M=48 from 60,000 to 600,000 changes the price by 0.124.  The targeted Figure 5 benchmark check at M=100 and N=500,000 gives K=100 price 10.914; across strikes its relative errors remain between 8.16% and 12.22%. Against fresh M=500, N=1,200,000 benchmarks, the same M=100 prices have relative errors 7.98% for K=90 and 7.22% for K=100 (fresh benchmarks 7.276 and 10.179). The all-path versus ITM-only policy effect is at most 0.060. The forward European K=100 diagnostic moves from 11.203 at M=48 to 9.736 at M=1200, before any Bermudan regression. Hybrid grid changes are small by comparison, with maximum K=100 grid range 0.044; the default Hybrid K=100 price changes by 0.510 from M=48 to M=96 at N=60,000. The dominant source is therefore Euler time-discretization/full-truncation bias in the square-root variance case.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
Do not treat the large relative errors as a path-count failure or a benchmark-file mismatch. If this parameter case remains in the manuscript, it should be framed as a stress test showing sensitivity of the square-root case to the Euler time step, or rerun with a finer-step design and a discretization scheme better suited to the boundary.
